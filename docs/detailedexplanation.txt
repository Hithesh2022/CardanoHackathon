# Detailed Explanation: Privacy-Preserving Credit Scoring Problem & Solution

## Problem Statement Deep Dive

### • Millions lack access to fair credit

Many people around the world cannot get loans or credit cards, not because they are untrustworthy, but because the current system does not capture their full financial behavior. If someone doesn't have a bank history or credit card, traditional systems assume they are "risky," even if they pay rent, bills, or run a small business responsibly. So, millions are unfairly denied credit.

### • Centralized scoring = biased

Credit scores are controlled by a few large organizations. Their scoring models can be biased, because they rely on limited data and outdated methods. For example, people from certain income groups, regions, or backgrounds may get lower scores—not based on their real ability to repay but due to structural bias in the system.

### • Traditional bureaus exclude users with limited data

If someone doesn't have a long financial history—like students, gig workers, new employees, or rural populations—the system doesn't know how to judge them. This "thin-file problem" means they don't get a score at all, making them invisible to lenders and blocking access to credit.

### • Insecure storage of personal info

Credit bureaus store massive amounts of sensitive data (PAN, Aadhar, bank details, past loans). When all this data is stored in one place (centralized systems), it becomes a big target for hackers. There have already been huge data breaches exposing millions of people's information.

### • No user control over financial identity

Users cannot decide who accesses their data, how long they store it, or how it is used. Once a bureau has your data, you have almost no control. You can't see how your score is calculated, can't correct errors easily, and can't stop them from sharing your info with financial institutions.

## How We Solve This Using Cardano Contracts

### High-Level Design (Components & Solutions)

#### 1. Self-Sovereign Identity (SSI) + Verifiable Credentials

**Technology**: Atala PRISM / Cardano DID wallets

**What it does**: Users own and control identity & credentials (income proofs, rent receipts, utility payments, KYC). Users present signed verifiable credentials to smart contracts instead of handing over raw documents.

**Problem solved**: 
- ✅ No user control over financial identity
- ✅ Insecure centralized storage

**How it works**:
- User creates a DID (Decentralized Identifier) wallet
- Attestors (employers, banks, utilities) issue verifiable credentials
- User keeps credentials in their wallet
- User selectively shares only what's needed
- No central database of personal info

#### 2. Off-Chain Data Attestations + Decentralized Oracles

**Technology**: Charli3 / Orcfax oracle networks

**What it does**: Many data points (telco bills, merchant receipts, mobile money history) live off-chain. Trusted attestors (banks, payroll providers, utilities, landlords, fintechs) issue signed credentials or push attestations to an oracle network that can relay hashed/aggregated data on-chain when needed.

**Problem solved**:
- ✅ Traditional bureaus exclude users with limited data
- ✅ Centralized scoring = biased

**How it works**:
- Multiple data sources (not just banks)
- Oracles bring real-world signals to blockchain
- Aggregated data (privacy-preserving)
- Wider data sources = fairer scoring

#### 3. Plutus Smart Contracts for Credit Logic & Consent

**Technology**: Cardano Plutus smart contracts (Aiken validator in our case)

**What it does**: Encodes consent records, scoring rules, token issuance, and dispute resolution on-chain.

**Problem solved**:
- ✅ Centralized scoring = biased
- ✅ No user control over financial identity
- ✅ Millions lack access to fair credit

**How it works**:
- Deterministic, auditable scoring rules
- On-chain consent tracking (who approved what data share, when)
- Transparent scoring logic
- Users can dispute and request re-evaluation
- Lenders see verifiable results without accessing raw data

**Our Implementation**: `score_proof.ak` validator with:
- ScoreDatum: stores owner PKH, score hash, bucket, nonce, expiry
- RevealBucket redeemer: selective disclosure (prove range, not exact score)
- UpdateScore redeemer: owner can refresh with new proofs

#### 4. Tokenized Credit Identity / Reputation

**Technology**: Cardano native tokens / NFTs

**What it does**: Issue a non-transferrable native token or NFT that represents a user's credit profile or credit-band (e.g., CREDIT-Grade-A NFT or a fungible "credit score" token).

**Problem solved**:
- ✅ Millions lack access to fair credit
- ✅ Traditional bureaus exclude users with limited data

**How it works**:
- Score represented as on-chain token/NFT
- Lenders read token metadata instead of raw data
- Portable identity (works across platforms)
- Immediate credit identity for thin-file users
- Native tokens are ledger-native and efficient

**Our Implementation**: Backend generates score buckets (0-4) stored in datum:
- Bucket 0: 300-499
- Bucket 1: 500-649
- Bucket 2: 650-749
- Bucket 3: 750-849
- Bucket 4: 850+

#### 5. Privacy Layer: ZK Proofs & Selective Disclosure

**Technology**: Zero-knowledge proofs (ZK), Halo2 → Plutus verifier integration

**What it does**: Users can prove statements like "income ≥ ₹30,000" or "on-time rent last 12 months" without revealing full paystubs.

**Problem solved**:
- ✅ Insecure storage of personal info
- ✅ No user control over financial identity

**How it works**:
- User generates ZK proof locally
- Proof proves a fact without revealing underlying data
- Lender verifies proof on-chain or via verifier contract
- No PII exposed to lender or blockchain

**Our Implementation**: Score bucket system is first step toward selective disclosure:
- Prove you're in bucket 3 (750-849) without revealing exact score (e.g., 782)
- Validator checks bucket match, expiry, and owner signature

#### 6. Encrypted Off-Chain Storage + On-Chain Pointers

**Technology**: IPFS/Arweave + encrypted storage with on-chain hashes

**What it does**: Store full documents encrypted in user-controlled storage and put only encrypted digests or pointers + consent receipts on Cardano.

**Problem solved**:
- ✅ Insecure storage of personal info
- ✅ No user control over financial identity

**How it works**:
- Raw documents stay off-chain (encrypted)
- Only hash/pointer goes on-chain
- User controls encryption keys
- Reduces attack surface (no central honeypot)

**Our Implementation**: Backend stores:
- Score hash (SHA-256 of score + session + nonce)
- Nonce for privacy (16-byte random)
- Capsule ID and tx hash as pointers

#### 7. Governance & Dispute Resolution

**Technology**: Smart contract hooks + DAO governance (planned)

**What it does**: Users can dispute attestations, request re-evaluation, and appeal decisions with on-chain audit trail.

**Problem solved**:
- ✅ Centralized scoring = biased
- ✅ No user control over financial identity

**How it works**:
- Dispute hooks in smart contracts
- Time-bounded appeals process
- Multi-stakeholder governance
- Slashing for bad attestations
- Transparent audit trail

## User Experience Flow

### 1. User Onboarding
- User creates a DID wallet (Atala PRISM)
- Requests verifiable credentials from employers, banks, utilities, marketplaces
- Attestors issue VC (signed JSON-LD / verifiable credential) to wallet

### 2. Data Sharing
- User picks which proofs to share
- Gives consent via Plutus contract (on-chain consent transaction)
- Contract records consent + pointer to encrypted evidence

### 3. Score Computation
- Plutus scoring contract consumes attestations
- Either provided from wallet or via oracle aggregated feeds
- Computes score deterministically
- Mints non-transferable "credit badge" token or updates score metadata

### 4. Lender Verification
- Lenders query the token/score
- Or request ZK proof (e.g., "score ≥ X") from user
- They never see raw documents
- ZK verifier contract verifies proof before loan underwriting

## Smart Contract / Technical Architecture

### Actors
- **User (holder)**: Owns DID wallet, controls data sharing
- **Attestor (issuer)**: Provides verifiable credentials (employer, bank, utility)
- **Oracle nodes**: Relay off-chain data on-chain
- **ScoringContract (Plutus)**: Our Aiken validator
- **Lender (verifier/consumer)**: Requests proofs, verifies credentials

### Our ScoringContract (Aiken Validator) Interface

**Functions**:

1. **submitConsent(did, attestationPointer, ttl)**
   - Records consent signature + pointer
   - Tracks who approved what data share

2. **submitAttestation(attestorSig, attestationHash)**
   - Verifies attestor signature
   - Stores hashed attestation

3. **computeScore(did, attestationHashes[])**
   - Deterministic scoring function
   - Applies fairness guard
   - Maps to bucket (0-4)

4. **mintBadge(did, scoreBand)**
   - Mints non-transferable native token/NFT to DID address
   - Represents credit band

5. **requestZKProof(did, predicate)**
   - Verifies ZK proof (off-chain with on-chain result)
   - Releases info to lender only if proof valid

**Our Implementation**: `score_proof.ak` provides:
- **ScoreDatum**: owner PKH, score_hash, score_bucket, nonce, expires_at
- **RevealBucket redeemer**: Selective disclosure with validation
- **UpdateScore redeemer**: Owner can refresh score

✔ Value doesn't fluctuate like crypto
✔ Perfect for loans, repayments, and EMI
✔ Works globally
✔ Transparent & auditable

**Create native token on Cardano**:
- CREDITX
- FLO-POINTS

## Regulatory & Practical Considerations

### 1. KYC/Consumer Credit Rules
- Vary by jurisdiction
- Incorporate off-chain KYC attestors
- Allow hybrid (traditional bureau data + Cardano badges)

### 2. Oracle & Attestor Trust
- Must be reputable and economically incentivized
- Include slashing/dispute mechanics
- Multi-signature requirements
- Stake-based attestation

### 3. Gas/Fee Design
- Frequent on-chain writes are costly
- Use batch commitments
- Consider side-chains or L2 solutions for high throughput
- Optimize datum size

### 4. Privacy Regulations (GDPR, etc.)
- Right to be forgotten (off-chain data deletion)
- Data minimization (only hashes on-chain)
- Consent management (on-chain consent records)
- Audit trails for compliance
